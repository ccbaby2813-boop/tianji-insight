# Quora 回答 1

## 问题
Why do intelligent educated people believe in systems like astrology and the I Ching?

## 回答内容（直接复制粘贴）

Great question. I think it comes down to three things that rational, educated people actually find compelling:

### 1. It's not fortune-telling — it's a decision framework

The I Ching (Book of Changes) is often misrepresented as a mystical oracle, but at its core it's a 3,000-year-old decision-making system. You don't "ask the universe your future." You frame a real problem, generate a hexagram through a random process (yarrow stalks or coins), and then use that hexagram as a structured prompt for self-reflection.

Think of it like a Rorschach test or a writing exercise. The value isn't in the hexagram being "right" — it's in forcing you to approach a problem from a completely unfamiliar angle.

### 2. The 64 hexagrams are actually a sophisticated personality system

This is what most people miss. The 64 hexagrams aren't random symbols — each one describes a complete archetype with:
- Core strengths and weaknesses
- Growth patterns
- How it handles conflict
- How it relates to other types

That's 64 personality types with 6 layers of nuance. Compare that to MBTI's 16 types or astrology's 12 signs. The I Ching system is far more granular, and it's been refined over 30 centuries.

When I first calculated my birth hexagram (the hexagram that corresponds to your birth date), I was shocked at how accurately it described my patterns of behavior — not in a vague "you're an introvert" way, but in specific, actionable ways.

### 3. It works as a mirror, not a crystal ball

Here's why intelligent people keep coming back: the I Ching doesn't predict your future. It reflects your current blind spots.

Modern psychology talks about "cognitive biases" — we all have blind spots. The I Ching, with its extremely symbolic and metaphorical language, forces you to translate abstract images into your own life context. That translation process is where the insight happens.

It's similar to journaling or therapy — you're not getting new information from outside, you're unlocking information that was already inside you but you couldn't see.

---

I should also note: I don't think the I Ching is "supernatural." I think it's an incredibly sophisticated piece of classical Chinese philosophy that modern psychology is only now catching up to. The fact that it's survived 3,000 years tells you something about how useful it is.
