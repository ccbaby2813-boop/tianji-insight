Spring Summer Autumn Winter · Seasonal Wellness · Based on Huangdi Neijing
The 24 Solar Terms is a traditional Chinese calendar system used to guide agricultural activities, representing the accumulated wisdom of the Chinese people. Each solar term has unique climate characteristics and wellness要点. Seasonal wellness means adapting your diet, lifestyle, exercise, and emotions to the changing seasons to achieve physical and mental health.
Spring (Wood · Liver)
In spring, the three months are called the period of unfolding. Heaven and earth together give birth to all things, and the myriad things flourish. Sleep late and rise early, walk widely in the courtyard, let your hair hang loose and relax your body, so that your mind may come to life.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the scientific basis for 24 solar terms wellness?

24 solar terms wellness is based on the Huangdi Neijing (Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon), specifically the 'Four Qi Regulating Spirit' chapter. Modern research confirms that seasonal changes significantly affect human circadian rhythms, hormone levels, immune function, and mental health. The core of seasonal wellness is aligning with natural rhythms to maintain harmony between body, mind, and environment.

How does wellness focus differ by solar term?

Spring focuses on soothing the liver and regulating qi - eat spring sprouts and chives, sleep early rise early, exercise more. Summer focuses on nourishing the heart and calming the spirit - eat light meals, avoid overexertion. Autumn focuses on moistening the lungs and preventing dryness - eat pears and tremella, sleep early rise early, gather your spirit. Winter focuses on tonifying the kidneys and storing essence - eat warming foods, sleep early rise late, keep warm.

Do I need to strictly follow solar terms wellness?

Solar terms wellness provides general wellness recommendations. Each person should adjust based on their individual constitution, health conditions, and lifestyle. If you have specific health concerns, consult a healthcare professional. The core is developing healthy habits, not strictly following rules.

How should I adjust my diet by season?

Spring: eat pungent, sweet, and dispersing foods (chives, cilantro, peanuts), avoid sour and astringent. Summer: eat heat-clearing and dampness-removing foods (mung beans, watermelon, bitter melon), avoid greasy and spicy. Autumn: eat yin-nourishing and lung-moistening foods (pears, tremella, honey), avoid spicy and dry-heating. Winter: eat warming and essence-storing foods (lamb, walnuts, chestnuts), avoid raw and cold.
