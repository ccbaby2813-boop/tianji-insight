Judgment & Image

Judgment (卦辞)
Return. Success. Going out and coming in without error. Friends come without blame. To and fro goes the way. On the seventh day comes return. It furthers one to have somewhere to go.
Image (象辞)
Thunder in the earth: the image of Return. Thus the ancient kings closed the passes at the solstice. Merchants and strangers did not go about, and the ruler did not travel over the provinces.

Personality Deep Dive

Your core is a seed—no matter how deep it's buried in the soil, it can push through rocks and grow. The defining trait of the Returning type is "refusing to give up": not being unafraid of losing, but always able to start over after a loss. Inner Zhen (Thunder) = there is a relentless impulse in your heart, like the first muffled thunder of winter, faint but never gone. Outer Kun (Earth) = you seem easygoing on the surface, even a little passive, but deep down you have an unyielding "you can't knock me down" grit. In your friends' eyes, you're the one who "how did you get up again"—knocked down, denied, rubbed into the ground by life, then quietly standing up, dusting yourself off, and continuing on.

Emotional & Relationship Patterns

In relationships, you are "slow to warm up but enduring"—you don't rush in with love at first sight, but once you start, you don't let go easily. Inner Zhen, Outer Kun: your feelings are like spring thunder—calm on the surface, but stirring underground. Earth overcomes Water, so you tend to be restrained in love; you won't readily say "I miss you," but you prove "I'm always here" through actions. The core of Return is "coming back"—you are most likely to rekindle after a breakup, speak first after a cold war, and quietly cook a meal after a fight.

Strengths & Growth Blind Spots

Innate Talent
Your gift is "restart"—you can begin again on things others have given up on, and you do it better than the first time. "Return on the seventh day" isn't superstition; it's a rhythm: every failure adds experience points, and every restart is steadier than the last. Goujian (the Yue king who lay on firewood and tasted gall for ten years before turning the tables) and Chu Shijian (the entrepreneur who started over at 74) are your spiritual kin.
Growth Blind Spot
Your blind spot is "repetition"—you may fall into the same pit several times. "To and fro goes the way" is both resilience and inertia: you tend to remain fixated on the same person and stubbornly pursue the same path to the end. Sometimes you should switch roads, but you're still spinning in place. The Image says "the ancient kings closed the passes at the solstice"—on the winter solstice, they stayed inside and were still. You're the same: not every fall requires an immediate get-up; sometimes sitting quietly for a while, getting clear on your direction, and then walking on is the better move.

Frequently Asked Questions about Hexagram 24 Fu

Quick answers about Fu personality, career, compatibility, and fortune

What is the personality of someone with Hexagram Fu?

Hexagram Fu represents the returning one finding renewal. People with this hexagram typically have distinctive traits and behavioral patterns, showing a unique style in relationships and career development. Understanding your hexagram personality helps you leverage strengths and address blind spots.

What careers suit Hexagram Fu?

People with Hexagram Fu often excel in transformation, restart, recovery, second entrepreneurship. Choosing a career that matches your hexagram traits increases the likelihood of fulfillment and sustained growth. Of course, career choices should also consider personal interests, abilities, and market opportunities.

Which hexagram is most compatible with Fu?

Hexagram Fu pairs best with Bo (silence before return). The two hexagrams complement or resonate with each other in personality, making it easier to understand one another and build rapport in relationships and partnerships. Compatibility is a reference point; real relationships require mutual effort and commitment.

Is Hexagram Fu auspicious or inauspicious for fortune-telling?

Hexagram Fu: auspicious: one yang returns, life reemerges. In the I Ching, auspiciousness is not absolute; it indicates the attitude and action appropriate for a given situation. Even an inauspicious hexagram can be navigated safely by upholding correctness and acting cautiously. Even an auspicious hexagram can lead to misfortune if one becomes complacent.
