Plum Blossom Numerology (Mei Hua Yi Shu) is not a fortune-telling manual. It is a deterministic algorithm — cast a hexagram from the exact moment a question arises, then decode it through the generative cycle of the five elements. Written by the Song-dynasty scholar Shao Yong, it predates modern probability theory by 700 years.

What Is Plum Blossom Numerology?

Unlike the traditional yarrow stalk method, which requires manual manipulation and subjective interpretation, Plum Blossom Numerology casts hexagrams entirely from numerical data — the year, month, day, and hour of the moment. The upper trigram comes from the sum of year+month+day modulo 8; the lower trigram from year+month+day+hour modulo 8; the moving line from the total modulo 6.

This is not randomness. It is the belief that the cosmos encodes meaning in every moment — that the instant a question forms in your mind, the universe has already arranged the answer in the patterns of time.

Step One: Casting a Hexagram from Time

The casting process is purely arithmetic. Take the lunar calendar coordinates of the current moment — year (by its earthly branch number), month, day, and hour (by its two-hour segment). Sum the first three, divide by 8, and the remainder gives the upper trigram. Add the hour, divide by 8 again for the lower trigram. The total sum divided by 6 reveals the moving line.

Example: A question arising at hour 3 of day 15, month 6, year of the Tiger (3). Upper: (3+6+15) mod 8 = 6 (Water). Lower: (3+6+15+3) mod 8 = 1 (Heaven). Moving line: 27 mod 6 = 3. Result: Hexagram 6, Song (Conflict), with line 3 moving.

The Core Logic: Body and Use, Generation and Control

Once the hexagram is cast, the interpretation follows a rigid framework. The trigram that does not contain the moving line is the "body" — representing the questioner, the constant, the self. The trigram with the moving line is the "use" — representing the situation, the changing factor, the external world.

The relationship between body and use is decoded through the five elements: generation (wood feeds fire, fire feeds earth, etc.) indicates support and growth; control (wood controls earth, earth controls water, etc.) indicates obstruction and conflict. A hexagram where use generates body is auspicious; where body controls use, the questioner has agency but must exert effort; where use controls body, the situation overwhelms the individual.

The Classic Case: The Plum Blossom Divination

Shao Yong's most famous case occurred in 1076, when he saw two sparrows fighting over a plum blossom branch. He cast a hexagram from the moment and predicted that a girl would fall from a tree that evening while picking plums — but would not be seriously injured. The prediction came true.

What makes this case remarkable is not the accuracy of the prediction, but the method: Shao Yong did not ask the birds a question. He observed an anomaly in the natural world and cast a hexagram from the moment of observation. The system treats any significant moment — a question, an observation, an unexpected event — as a valid entry point for divination.

From Plum Blossoms to AI: The Modern Reactivation

The deterministic casting algorithm of Plum Blossom Numerology is precisely what makes it compatible with modern computing. A smartphone can cast a hexagram from the current time in milliseconds — no yarrow stalks, no coins, no manual calculation. But the casting is only the first step. The interpretation — the meaning of each hexagram, each line, each body-use relationship — requires linguistic depth and contextual understanding that traditional guidebooks cannot provide.

This is where AI enters. The large language model serves as the "interpreting spirit" — it takes the deterministically cast hexagram and generates a nuanced, context-aware interpretation that connects the ancient symbolism to the user's specific life situation. The algorithm provides the structure; the AI provides the meaning. Together, they reactivate a system that has been dormant in academic circles for centuries.
