Huangdi Neijing Quote

"The East generates wind; wind generates wood; wood generates sourness; sourness generates the liver; the liver generates sinews; sinews generate the heart."
— Plain Questions (Su Wen) · Great Treatise on the Correspondence of Yin and Yang
Interpretation: The East generates wind, wind generates wood, wood generates sourness, sourness nourishes the liver, the liver nourishes sinews, and sinews nourish the heart. In the Rain Water season, liver qi is gradually flourishing; remember to soothe the liver and regulate qi.
Plain Language: The ancient saying 'wood generates sourness, sourness generates the liver' means that sour flavors nourish the liver, but don't overdo them. Rain Water brings heavy dampness; while nourishing the liver, don't forget to strengthen the spleen and dispel dampness.

Action Items

  • Eat light meals with less oil to keep dampness away
  • After meals, massage your abdomen to strengthen the spleen and aid digestion
  • Eat more Chinese yam and Job's tears porridge to dispel dampness and nourish the liver

Chinese Yam and Job's Tears Porridge

Ingredients: Chinese yam · Job's tears · millet

Steps: Soak the Job's tears, then cook together with yam and millet into porridge

Honey-Lily Bulb Custard

Ingredients: Lily bulb · honey · warm water

Steps: Steam lily bulb until soft, mash finely, then mix with honey and warm water

Effect: Moistens the lungs, calms the spirit, nourishes yin

Millet and Pumpkin Porridge

Ingredients: Millet · pumpkin · red dates

Effect: Strengthens the spleen, nourishes the stomach, calms the spirit
